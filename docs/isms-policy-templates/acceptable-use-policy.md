# Acceptable Use Policy

**Document Reference:** ISMS-POL-001
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Template
**Review Cycle:** Annual
**Policy Owner:** [Information Security Manager]
**Date:** [YYYY-MM-DD]

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines the acceptable use of information systems, devices, and data owned or managed by [ORGANISATION NAME]. It establishes the standards of behaviour expected of all users to protect the organisation's information assets, ensure regulatory compliance, and maintain the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of data and systems.

This policy supports [ORGANISATION NAME]'s Information Security Management System (ISMS) and is aligned with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All permanent employees, contractors, temporary staff, and volunteers of [ORGANISATION NAME].
- Any third party granted access to [ORGANISATION NAME] systems, networks, or data.
- All information systems, devices, applications, and data owned, leased, or managed by [ORGANISATION NAME], whether accessed on-premises or remotely.
- All locations, including [ORGANISATION NAME] premises, home working environments, and any other remote working location.

This policy applies regardless of whether systems are accessed via [ORGANISATION NAME]-provided devices or personally owned devices where [ORGANISATION NAME] has authorised such access.

---

## 3. Policy Statements

### 3.1 Personal Use of Company Systems

3.1.1 Incidental personal use of [ORGANISATION NAME] information systems (including internet access and email) is permitted provided it is minimal, does not interfere with business activities, does not incur unreasonable costs, and complies with all other provisions of this policy.

3.1.2 Personal use must not involve the storage, processing, or transmission of personal data belonging to third parties on [ORGANISATION NAME] systems, except where required in the course of legitimate work duties.

3.1.3 Users must not use [ORGANISATION NAME] systems for personal business activities, commercial gain, or activities that create a conflict of interest with [ORGANISATION NAME].

3.1.4 [ORGANISATION NAME] reserves the right to monitor, record, and audit all activity on its systems, networks, and devices in accordance with applicable UK legislation, including the Investigatory Powers Act 2016 and the Data Protection Act 2018. Users have no expectation of privacy when using [ORGANISATION NAME] systems for personal purposes.

### 3.2 Prohibited Activities

3.2.1 The following activities are strictly prohibited on [ORGANISATION NAME] systems, networks, and devices:

- Accessing, downloading, storing, or distributing material that is illegal under UK law, including but not limited to material that is indecent, obscene, defamatory, or constitutes harassment.
- Attempting to circumvent or disable security controls, access controls, monitoring systems, or data loss prevention tools.
- Accessing, modifying, or deleting data, systems, or accounts to which the user has not been granted explicit authorisation.
- Installing, running, or distributing malware, exploits, hacking tools, or other malicious software.
- Using [ORGANISATION NAME] systems to conduct denial-of-service attacks, port scanning, or any other form of unauthorised network probing.
- Sharing account credentials with any other person, including colleagues.
- Using another person's credentials to access systems, even with that person's knowledge and consent.
- Connecting unauthorised devices, systems, or services to the [ORGANISATION NAME] network without prior approval from IT.
- Downloading, copying, or transmitting large volumes of [ORGANISATION NAME] data to personal or unapproved storage, except where required for authorised business purposes.

3.2.2 Violations of these prohibitions may result in disciplinary action up to and including dismissal, and may be reported to law enforcement authorities where a criminal act has occurred or is suspected.

### 3.3 Internet and Email Use

3.3.1 Internet access is provided for business purposes. Users must exercise judgement when accessing websites and must not visit sites that are known or suspected to host malicious content, illegal material, or content inappropriate to a professional environment.

3.3.2 Users must not attempt to bypass web content filtering or proxy controls using VPNs, Tor, or other circumvention methods.

3.3.3 Email must be used professionally and in a manner consistent with [ORGANISATION NAME]'s values and legal obligations. Users must not send threatening, discriminatory, harassing, or otherwise offensive communications via email or any other [ORGANISATION NAME] messaging system.

3.3.4 Sensitive or confidential information must not be sent via unencrypted email to external recipients unless explicitly approved by the Information Security Manager and accompanied by appropriate controls (e.g. encryption, password protection, or use of a secure file transfer service).

3.3.5 Users must exercise caution with all email attachments and links, particularly from unknown or unexpected senders. Suspected phishing emails must be reported immediately using the designated reporting mechanism.

3.3.6 Bulk personal email communications must not be sent from [ORGANISATION NAME] email systems.

### 3.4 Removable Media

3.4.1 The use of removable media (including USB drives, external hard drives, SD cards, and optical media) on [ORGANISATION NAME] systems is subject to prior approval from the Information Security Manager.

3.4.2 Where approved, only [ORGANISATION NAME]-issued or IT-approved removable media may be used. Personally owned removable media must not be connected to [ORGANISATION NAME] systems.

3.4.3 All data stored on approved removable media must be encrypted using an approved encryption standard (minimum AES-256).

3.4.4 Removable media containing [ORGANISATION NAME] data must be stored securely when not in use and must not be left unattended in public or shared spaces.

3.4.5 Lost or stolen removable media containing [ORGANISATION NAME] data must be reported immediately to the IT Security team and the Information Security Manager. This may constitute a personal data breach requiring assessment under UK GDPR.

### 3.5 Remote Working

3.5.1 Users working remotely must connect to [ORGANISATION NAME] systems using approved and secure methods, including the corporate VPN or other approved remote access technology, unless specifically exempted by the Information Security Manager.

3.5.2 Users must ensure that their home working environment does not permit unauthorised persons (including family members) to view [ORGANISATION NAME] information on-screen or access [ORGANISATION NAME] devices.

3.5.3 [ORGANISATION NAME]-issued devices must not be used by any person other than the authorised user, including family members or friends.

3.5.4 Users must lock their devices using the approved screen lock (minimum 5-minute timeout) when leaving them unattended, whether at home or in any other remote working location.

3.5.5 The use of public Wi-Fi networks to access [ORGANISATION NAME] systems is only permitted when connected via the corporate VPN or an equivalent approved secure connection.

### 3.6 Social Media

3.6.1 Users must not post, share, or disclose [ORGANISATION NAME] confidential information, client data, commercially sensitive information, or internal discussions on any social media platform or public forum.

3.6.2 Users must not make statements on social media that could be construed as representing the official position of [ORGANISATION NAME] unless they have been explicitly authorised to do so.

3.6.3 Users who identify themselves as employees of [ORGANISATION NAME] on personal social media accounts must make clear that any views expressed are their own and do not represent those of [ORGANISATION NAME].

3.6.4 Users must not engage in social media behaviour that could bring [ORGANISATION NAME] into disrepute or that constitutes harassment, discrimination, or bullying directed at colleagues, clients, or competitors.

### 3.7 Reporting Obligations

3.7.1 All users must report suspected or actual information security incidents, including phishing attempts, malware infections, unauthorised access, data loss, or policy violations, to the IT Security team or Information Security Manager as soon as practicable and no later than two hours after suspicion arises.

3.7.2 Users must report the loss or theft of any [ORGANISATION NAME] device, credential, or removable media containing [ORGANISATION NAME] data immediately upon discovery.

3.7.3 Users must not attempt to investigate, remediate, or conceal a suspected security incident. Reporting and investigation is the responsibility of the IT Security team.

3.7.4 Reports may be made in confidence and [ORGANISATION NAME] will not penalise staff who report incidents in good faith, even where the user has unintentionally contributed to the incident.

### 3.8 Software Installation

3.8.1 Users must not install software, browser extensions, plugins, or applications on [ORGANISATION NAME] devices without prior approval from IT. This includes freeware, shareware, and open-source software.

3.8.2 Software procurement and installation must follow [ORGANISATION NAME]'s software approval process. Approved software is maintained on the software asset register.

3.8.3 AI-assisted tools, productivity applications, and cloud-based software-as-a-service (SaaS) tools that process [ORGANISATION NAME] data or connect to [ORGANISATION NAME] systems require review and approval by the Information Security Manager before use.

3.8.4 Users must not connect personally owned devices to the [ORGANISATION NAME] corporate network without prior approval. Where approved, personally owned devices are subject to minimum security requirements defined by IT (including up-to-date operating system patching and endpoint security software).

## 4. Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|------|-----------------|
| All Staff and Contractors | Read, understand, and comply with this policy. Report suspected violations or incidents promptly. Complete required security awareness training. |
| Information Security Manager | Own and maintain this policy. Ensure the policy is reviewed annually and updated as required. Approve exceptions. Investigate reported violations. |
| IT Department | Implement technical controls to support this policy (web filtering, DLP, endpoint management). Manage the software approval process. Respond to reported incidents. |
| Line Managers | Ensure team members have read and understood this policy. Report suspected violations by team members to the Information Security Manager. |
| HR Department | Incorporate policy acceptance into the onboarding process. Support disciplinary processes where policy violations occur. |
| Senior Management | Provide visible support for the policy. Approve significant exceptions and resource decisions. |

---

## 5. Exceptions Process

Requests for exceptions to any provision of this policy must be submitted in writing to the Information Security Manager. Exceptions will be considered on a case-by-case basis, subject to a risk assessment. Approved exceptions will be documented with:

- A clear description of the exception and the justification.
- A named exception owner and approver.
- A time limit and review date (exceptions are not granted indefinitely).
- Any compensating controls required.

Exceptions that introduce significant risk must be escalated to senior management for approval. All approved exceptions are recorded in the ISMS risk register.
